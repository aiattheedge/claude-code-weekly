# Intro: the minutes problem

Hi, welcome to AI At The Edge. Think about your last big meeting. Three decisions, five action items, and a week later nobody agrees on what was said. That's why transcription is so valuable. Accurate minutes, searchable decisions, owners on every action item, and nobody stuck taking notes instead of thinking.

# From note taker to member

But a transcript is still about after the meeting. This week Anthropic took the next step with Claude Meetings, in beta for Team and Enterprise plans. Claude joins your Google Meet as a visible participant. You ask it something out loud or in the chat, and it builds the doc or the whiteboard with you, during the call. When the meeting ends, the notes, the transcript and everything it made are on one page. Claude stops being the note taker and becomes a member of the meeting.

# Why big companies say no

So why don't large companies just turn this on? At a large regulated company, I've seen transcription locked down hard. You need a full business case, and you get it for a day or less, one time. That's not paranoia. Here's what legal is weighing.

Consent. Federal law needs one person's okay to record. About a dozen states want everyone's, and on a call across states the strictest rule usually wins.

Lawsuits. AI note takers are being sued, and this year a federal judge let a class action against one move forward. A visible bot in the meeting wasn't enough to prove people knew.

Records. A transcript is a business record. It can be pulled into a lawsuit, and regulators have fined financial firms billions over records kept in the wrong place.

And privilege and privacy. One lawyer on the call, or one customer's details said out loud, and that transcript is now a problem.

# What Anthropic built in

Claude Meetings handles some of this. It posts a notice when it joins. On Enterprise plans it stays out of calls with outside guests by default, and leaves if one joins. It asks each person before using their connected apps. It saves a transcript, not audio. And Anthropic says it plainly: make sure everyone is okay with being transcribed. One honest catch: during the beta, it isn't offered to companies on zero data retention or HIPAA-ready setups. That's a lot of regulated companies.

# Agents inside your walls

So the bigger answer is where the agent runs. Companies can now run Claude Managed Agents inside their own security perimeter, in their own cloud or on their own servers, in private beta. Sandboxes, credentials and session history stay with you, and you pick who serves the model. Four rules: code runs in a sandbox, apart from the agent and its secrets. Credentials never enter that sandbox. Each environment reaches only approved sites. And every step is recorded. That's the language legal, security and records teams already speak.

# How to start

If I were leading this, I'd start small. One internal meeting type, no outside guests, no lawyers, no HR. Tell everyone before Claude joins. Have a person approve the notes before they become the record. Decide up front how long transcripts are kept. Then bring legal real examples, not hypotheticals.
